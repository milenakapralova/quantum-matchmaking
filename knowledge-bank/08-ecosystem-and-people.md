## Where the EQTA sits

Many tools already assess negative impacts of new technologies. The Data Protection Impact Assessment and the Human Rights and Algorithms Impact Assessment (IAMA) are the ones named. The EQTA does not replace them. They can be done in addition. The example: when quantum technology is used to develop better machine-learning models, the IAMA is the tool that looks specifically at negative effects of algorithms.

The added value claimed for the EQTA is an early and structured consideration of the impact of quantum technologies.

The EQTA was written for Quantum Delta Nederland and commissioned by the Centre for Quantum and Society. CQS works on ELSA tooling and research (ethical, legal, and societal aspects), on awareness, and on the societal issues quantum technology can contribute to. Going through the EQTA raises questions the document does not answer. CQS is the first port of call: cqs@quantumdelta.nl.

The preface is signed by Freeke Heijman (board member, QDNL), Bart Schermer (chairman of the working group), and Daniël Frijters (co-chair). Points of contact at QDNL and at the Centre for Quantum and Society are part of the text. The colophon adds that QDNL commissioned ECP to develop the EQTA, and thanks the working group led by Bart Schermer and Daniël Frijters.

## What QDNL was doing

CQS is part of QDNL. QDNL coordinates the Dutch quantum ecosystem. The activities listed:

**Awareness.** Quantum.Amsterdam, a networking organization, offers awareness training on the technical, economic, and societal impact, with a focus on computing. Quantum for Business is a field lab in the QDNL program, aimed at the European market of companies and organizations, to open access to knowledge and to parties to collaborate with.

**Co-creation sites.** The Centre for Quantum and Society is the central co-creation activity of the QDNL Quantum and Society program: a virtual and physical place where society, industry, government, and science work on applications for society. House of Quantum is to become an organization of national physical spaces in the ecosystem where members work, learn, and meet. The program has five hubs — Delta, Eindhoven, Leiden, Twente, and Amsterdam — where co-creation happens. The hub page is cited as the Delta Hub URL.

**Application development.** The Quantum Application Lab is a field lab where parties and quantum-technology providers develop useful quantum-computing applications for end users. That can include societal issues, in collaboration with CQS.

## Lines that recur across the group

These are the working group’s own formulations, useful as the document’s emphasis rather than as findings:

- Prepare now, while applications are still early, with end users and civil society.
- Look for processes that are simplified today only because computing power is lacking.
- Opportunities and undesirable effects both exist; lack of preparation is the risk.
- The Netherlands’ digital infrastructure has to stay secure while the technology arrives on uneven timelines.
- Ethics belongs in the design phase, not only after deployment.
- A risk-based legal-ethical framework, standards, certification, and lifecycle assessment are, for Kop, what responsible quantum innovation requires.

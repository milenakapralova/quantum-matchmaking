# Assessment checklist

Booklet pp. 6–8, 11–15, 35, 38–53, 68–71. Use this file to run the EQTA. Background for each block is in the linked files.

## Quick Scan

Aim: a fast picture of what quantum technology brings, which opportunities it offers, and which negative effects are plausible. The EQTA can end here.

1. **Technology.** A short orientation on quantum technology and timelines. Use [07-quantum-primer.md](07-quantum-primer.md).
2. **Applications.** What is in store if the technology is applied around the organization, or if the organization applies it itself? Use [06-examples.md](06-examples.md) and the QDNL map in [08-ecosystem-and-people.md](08-ecosystem-and-people.md).
3. **Description.** From those examples, outline the impact the organization will feel, and which applications are relevant. Include opportunities and potential threats.
4. **Stakeholders.** Who, broadly, is affected in and around the organization? Which experts matter?
5. **Dialogue.** Test that picture. What is needed to make the most of the possibilities and, where necessary, avoid negative impacts? The dialogue also decides whether the full EQTA is necessary. Method: [05-practice.md](05-practice.md).
6. **Decision.**
   - If the organization must act now, or must actively monitor, continue at Step 1.
   - If active guidance is not necessary, document and justify that decision (Step 6).

## Exploration

### Step 1 — Societal framework

Detail: [02-social-framework.md](02-social-framework.md).

**1. Perception.** Which groups must contribute, and what do they need to know?

- What ideas about quantum technology exist inside and outside the organization, and how might they affect the application?
- Which groups or individuals should be able to take part? What do they need to know?

**2. Socio-technical ecosystem.** What social and technical conditions does success require?

- What must the people who work with the technology be able to do, and does the way of working have to change?
- What technical ecosystem does the application assume, and how could changes in that environment improve it?
- Cover skills and knowledge, organizational processes and governance, and the meaning of the application for the technology the organization already uses.

**3. Stakeholders.** Who is affected, and how are they involved?

- Who is impacted, and in what way?
- How can those groups be involved in development, implementation, and evaluation, in advance and on a continuing basis?

**4. External dependencies and rules.** Do rules, procedures, professional codes, best practices, or standards need to change? Is cooperation required with chain partners, regulators, or political and administrative actors? See also Step 3.

- Which regulations apply?
- Which gaps call for new or modified frameworks?

**5. International context.** How does the application depend on international cooperation and geopolitical developments, and how can those dependencies be watched and influenced?

- Which international actors are involved, and which dependencies may arise?
- Do foreign actors have access to the application, and what follows from that?

### Step 2 — Technological framework

Detail: [03-technological-framework.md](03-technological-framework.md).

**6. Quality.** What quality requirements must the technology meet to deliver the stated goals and benefits? Include functionality, robustness, the ability to analyze and repair errors, security, flexibility, manageability, integration with other technology, and sustainability (origin and reuse of materials, energy). ISO 25010 is the suggested checklist. Certifications from the supplier are the usual way these issues are handled.

**7. Privacy, security, data, identity, authentication, and authorization.**

- Which technical principles protect privacy and give people real chances to consent and to stay in control?
- Which authentication and authorization mechanisms give the many data subjects control over collection and use?
- How will reliability and security of data be ensured?
- Quantum computing will need a lot of data, so parties must make data available to each other. Which data is shared, which stays competitive, which is confidential, and which is open? Is a management organization needed for security, privacy, and quality?
- How are the risks of greater interconnection and dependence on third parties managed?

**8. Future-proof investment and freedom of choice.** Which conditions keep later technical choices open and avoid locking the organization to one vendor’s pace and direction?

### Step 3 — Legal and ethical frameworks

Detail: [04-legal-ethical-framework.md](04-legal-ethical-framework.md).

**Law**

- International: fundamental rights and treaties.
- Domestic, sectoral, and industry-specific rules.
- Government oversight of finance, security, continuity, and accountability.
- Intellectual property, and technology-specific regimes (the document points at the AI Act and at restrictions on dissemination and use).
- Geopolitics: sovereignty, and limits on collaboration with people and companies.

**Ethics**

- How can quantum technology serve the values that matter to the organization?
- Which quantum-specific ethical framework fits, and does it rest on a clear method from a reliable source?

## In practice

### Step 4 — Stakeholder dialogue

Also the last step of the Quick Scan. Bring together:

- experts who can explain the opportunities and who know the technical, social, ethical, and legal frames;
- administrators who steer the organization;
- stakeholders who feel the impact and people who will work with the technology.

Purpose: describe tangible approaches for the organization, with their advantages and disadvantages, from those different perspectives. In the Quick Scan, the dialogue only has to show which aspects matter and whether a closer analysis is needed. After a full pass through Steps 1–3, the same dialogue brings the concerns and requirements together.

Use the Approach to Guidance Ethics ([05-practice.md](05-practice.md)):

1. Describe the application in its context, as concretely as possible.
2. Name everyone involved, the positive and negative effects on them, and the values at stake.
3. Name concrete measures that reduce negative effects or strengthen positive ones, inside the technology, in the context, or in how it is used.

### Step 5 — Trade-off

Compare the approaches from Step 4.

- **Effectiveness.** Will the action actually achieve the goal? How likely is that?
- **Necessity and subsidiarity.** Are there other ways to reach the goal? Is this the least intrusive means?
- **Proportionality.** Is the impact on people proportionate to the importance of the goal? A more important goal can justify more.

The nutshell (p. 15) names subsidiarity, proportionality, and necessity. The in-practice chapter (p. 70) adds effectiveness and treats necessity together with subsidiarity. Use the fuller set.

### Step 6 — Decision, record, justification

Write down the choices and the accountability for them. The record is the reference for design and further development, and it is how the reasoning can be shown later. Annex 2 is the documentation overview. The fill-in roadmap itself is a separate download from quantumdelta.nl (booklet pp. 102–103). It is not printed in full in this PDF.

### Step 7 — Feedback and periodic evaluation

Design the processes that get the application built and that check it produces the intended outcomes. Actively manage delivery of those outcomes. Because the application is likely still in development, periodically reopen the ethical and social justification. State how the feedback loop will work. Then plan the next review of the decision and of the measures taken.
